# Case Study

This case study is based on a fictionalized aviation company that operates airline services and relies heavily on information technology to support its business operations. The organization manages various information assets, including business applications, employee accounts, customer-related information, operational systems, network infrastructure, and cloud-based services.

As the organization's reliance on information technology continues to increase, maintaining the confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) of information becomes critical to business operations.

The Information Security function is responsible for maintaining the organization's Information Security Management System (ISMS/SMKI) and ensuring that applicable information security controls are properly implemented, monitored, and continuously improved.

To evaluate the effectiveness and conformity of the existing security management system, an internal audit based on ISO/IEC 27001:2022 is conducted.

The objective of the audit is to assess the implementation of applicable information security controls, identify gaps or nonconformities, evaluate associated risks, and provide recommendations for remediation and continual improvement.

# Scenario
## Scope

The audit scope covers the organization's Information Security Management System (ISMS/SMKI) and selected information security processes supporting business and IT operations.

The assessment includes:

Information security policies and procedures
Information assets and supporting infrastructure
User access and identity management
Network and endpoint security
Security monitoring and incident management
Vulnerability and risk management
Backup and business continuity
Information security awareness
Supplier and third-party security
Security event logging and monitoring
Applicable Annex A controls of ISO/IEC 27001:2022

The audit focuses on evaluating whether the defined controls are implemented, documented, monitored, and supported by appropriate evidence.

### Audit Objectives

The main objectives of the audit are to:

Assess the organization's conformity with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirements.
Evaluate the implementation of applicable Annex A controls.
Identify control gaps and potential nonconformities.
Assess information security risks associated with identified gaps.
Review the effectiveness of existing security processes.
Provide recommendations for remediation and continual improvement.

### Current Information Assets

The organization manages various information assets that support its business and operational activities, including:

Business and operational applications
Customer and employee information
User accounts and authentication credentials
Databases and information repositories
Network infrastructure
Servers and endpoints
Security monitoring systems
Email and collaboration platforms
Cloud services
Backup infrastructure
Security logs and audit records
Security policies, procedures, and documentation
Third-party and supplier services

These assets require appropriate security controls to protect them against unauthorized access, modification, disclosure, disruption, and loss.

# Risk Assessment
## Risk Description

The organization has established information security controls and an ISMS; however, periodic assessment is required to ensure that controls remain effective and aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirements.

Potential risks may arise from:

Incomplete implementation of security controls
Inconsistent documentation
Excessive user privileges
Inadequate access reviews
Insufficient monitoring
Delayed vulnerability remediation
Inadequate backup or recovery procedures
Lack of security awareness
Third-party security risks
Incomplete evidence of control implementation

If these risks are not appropriately addressed, they may affect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of organizational information.

## Risk Impact

The potential impact of an information security incident may include:

Unauthorized disclosure of information
Data modification or corruption
Service disruption
Operational interruption
Financial losses
Regulatory or contractual consequences
Reputational damage

For an aviation organization, disruption to critical IT systems may also affect operational continuity and the availability of business services.

## Audit Assessment Approach

The audit evaluates each applicable control based on available evidence.

The assessment status can be categorized as:

Status	Description
Compliant	Control is implemented and supported by sufficient evidence.
Partially Compliant	Control has been implemented but requires improvement or additional evidence.
Non-Compliant	Required control has not been adequately implemented.
Not Applicable	Control is not applicable to the defined audit scope.

Evidence may include:

Policies
SOPs
Configuration records
Access review records
Security logs
Monitoring dashboards
Audit reports
Risk assessments
Incident records
Training records
Meeting/documentation records

# Recommendations

Based on the audit assessment, the organization should continuously improve its ISMS by:

Strengthening periodic access reviews and least-privilege implementation.
Improving vulnerability tracking and remediation monitoring.
Maintaining sufficient evidence for security controls.
Conducting periodic backup restoration/testing.
Enhancing security monitoring and incident response capabilities.
Reviewing third-party and supplier security requirements.
Maintaining regular information security awareness activities.
Periodically reviewing and updating security policies and procedures.
Monitoring corrective actions resulting from audit findings.
Applying continual improvement to the ISMS based on identified risks and audit results.

# Audit Conclusion
The internal audit provides an overview of the organization's current information security posture against applicable ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requirements and Annex A controls.
The assessment demonstrates that maintaining ISO/IEC 27001 compliance is not limited to having policies and procedures in place. The organization must also ensure that controls are implemented effectively, monitored, supported by evidence, periodically reviewed, and continuously improved.
The identified gaps should be prioritized based on their associated risk and potential impact on the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information assets.
